# Findings Summary — Analytics and Managerial Judgement in UK SMEs

## Central finding

The dissertation concludes that data-driven evidence and managerial judgement are **complementary and adaptively balanced**, not competing alternatives. Managers used numbers to detect patterns but relied on situational knowledge, customer contact and further checks to decide what those patterns meant.

## Five major themes

### Theme 1 — Data identifies patterns, but context gives them meaning

Sales figures, online metrics or transaction counts may show *what* happened but not *why*. Managers considered local events, customer needs, timing, weather and sector-specific circumstances before interpreting a change as good or bad news.

**Business implication:** Investigate context before making a decision from a KPI anomaly.

### Theme 2 — From attention to commercial outcomes: a hierarchy of evidence

Participants distinguished superficial signals such as likes, views or expressed interest from more decision-relevant evidence such as purchases, repeat customers, bookings, margins, retention and waste.

**Business implication:** Use the KPI that best reflects the commercial decision rather than the easiest metric to display.

### Theme 3 — Balancing data and judgement is adaptive, not fixed

Managers placed more weight on relevant, credible evidence when it was available, but relied more on judgement where data was incomplete, novel or unstable. Testing, additional observation and customer feedback helped manage uncertainty.

**Business implication:** Treat decisions as hypotheses that can be tested and revised.

### Theme 4 — SME context creates both agility and vulnerability

SMEs benefited from direct customer relationships and flexible decision-making. However, limited time, analytical capability and resources could constrain systematic measurement; concentrated ownership decisions might also introduce subjective bias.

**Business implication:** Choose light-weight, useful measures and involve more than one information source when feasible.

### Theme 5 — AI augments managerial capability but does not remove human control

Participants described AI-supported tools mainly as ways to improve capability or efficiency, while retaining human responsibility for interpretation, context, trust and accountability.

**Business implication:** Incorporate AI as decision support with appropriate review rather than as an unexamined decision-maker.

## Practical decision framework derived from the study

**Observe data → assess reliability → add customer/business context → compare with experience → test or act → review new evidence.**

This is a **qualitative interpretation of the findings**, not a validated predictive algorithm.

## Where these findings matter

- **Business analysis:** Stakeholder discovery and evaluating competing forms of evidence.
- **BI and reporting:** Designing metrics that reflect decisions, not only activity.
- **Marketing analytics:** Distinguishing engagement from commercially meaningful conversion.
- **Digital transformation:** Recognising adoption barriers and users' practical context.
- **AI governance:** Maintaining human oversight and accountability.

## Limitations

The analysis is based on nine purposively selected, self-reported interviews, including two comparative cases. It supports contextual insight rather than estimates that generalise to all UK SMEs. The project did not test whether implementing these recommendations improved company outcomes.
