# Research Aim, Question and Objectives

## Dissertation title

**Balancing Managerial Judgement and Data-Driven Decision-Making in Marketing: A Qualitative Study of UK Retail SMEs**

MSc Business Analytics, Newcastle University (2025/26).

## Background and problem

Retail SMEs increasingly have access to digital performance measures, customer transaction information, social-media analytics and AI-supported tools. Yet analytical output does not automatically translate into sound marketing choices. Managers must interpret evidence in situations shaped by changing customer behaviour, uncertain conditions and limited organisational resources.

The study focuses on **how analytical evidence and managerial judgement interact in practice** rather than assuming one replaces the other.

## Overall research aim

To explore how managers in UK retail SMEs balance data-driven decision-making with managerial judgement when making marketing decisions.

## Main research question

**How do managers in UK retail SMEs balance data-driven evidence with managerial judgement in marketing decision-making?**

## Research objectives

1. Examine how managers **interpret and evaluate different forms of marketing evidence**.
2. Explore how **experience and judgement influence decisions** when analytical evidence is limited or uncertain.
3. Investigate how **SME organisational context** shapes the use of data and judgement.
4. Consider the **emerging role of AI-supported tools** within managerial decision-making.

## Intended contribution

The project explores how decision-makers move between data, experience, context, testing and action. Its contribution is a context-sensitive account of decision-making that explains why useful analytics depends on interpretation and judgement, especially in resource-constrained organisations.

## Scope

Seven interview cases were drawn from the core retail SME context; two additional interviews provided comparative perspectives from service and wealth-management settings. Findings are qualitative and contextual, not population estimates.
